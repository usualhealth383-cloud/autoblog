## App name
Yakjido: medicines by symptom

## Short description
Pick a symptom: home care, Korean pharmacy picks and when to see a doctor.

## Full description
Choose where you feel unwell, and Yakjido shows you, step by step, what to do at home, which medicines to look for at a Korean pharmacy (how pain relievers, anti-inflammatories, muscle relaxants and stomach medicines are paired, and why each ingredient), and the signs that mean you should see a doctor. Key doses and advice show their sources and the month they were checked on every screen.

■ Start from your symptom
· 62 symptoms — headache, colds, indigestion, back pain, period pain, allergies, gum problems, tinnitus and more
· Urgent signs such as chest tightness or severe breathlessness come first — tick one and you see only how to call 119 (Korea's emergency number) instead of medicines
· One screen in order: things to do without medicine → what to pick at the pharmacy → when to see a doctor
· Turn on your own conditions (65 or older, pregnancy, breastfeeding, stomach ulcer, kidney disease, blood thinners) and medicines to be careful with are flagged first (it can't catch every risk — always tell the pharmacist when you buy medicine)
· Simple view: just a photo, an easy name and one line on how to take it

■ Medicine guide — ingredients first
· 153 commonly used medicines explained: how to take them, who shouldn't, and how many people in 100 get side effects
· Compare medicines in the same group: how anti-inflammatories differ in strain on the stomach and heart
· Can I take these together? Checks for overlapping ingredients in your medicine box, like acetaminophen hidden in cold medicines, and some risky combinations
· Includes Korean drug agency open data: 4,746 medicine summaries, 25,428 pill identifications and product approvals

■ Supplements — food first
· Start from "What's bothering you?" or "Who are you?" — signs that need a doctor more than a supplement come first
· 17 nutrients: which foods have them, what they're good for, and how much a day (2025 Korean Dietary Reference Intakes)

■ Every day
· Today's medicine card with one tap for "Taken", this week's dose bars and your streak
· Dose reminders (morning, lunch, evening, bedtime), refill reminders, and a reminder the day before a clinic visit
· Blood pressure log, children's dose calculator (by weight)
· Family alerts (optional): if no dose is recorded after the time has passed, family members you've approved get an alert — without medicine names
· Family chat (optional): short messages and "Taken" cards only between connected family members
· Send to parents: share the chosen medicines by KakaoTalk or text — they can show it to the pharmacist as is
· Four text sizes, dark mode, Korean, English, Spanish, Japanese and Chinese

■ Trustworthy
· 886 references and guidelines — pooled analyses, large comparative trials, clinical guidelines and Korean product labels
· Medicine information follows the Korean approved label; where overseas guidelines differ, both are shown
· Your medicines and records are stored on this phone first. Only if you log in and agree separately are they copied to a server in Seoul, Korea, so you can continue on a new phone
· No ads or analytics tools

Yakjido is not a medical device. It doesn't identify or cure any condition — it's reference guidance to help you choose and keep track of medicines, and it doesn't replace medical care. When you buy medicine, always tell the pharmacist about your conditions and the medicines you take, and see a doctor if symptoms continue. In an emergency, such as trouble breathing or confusion, call 119 at once.
